## Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme uses a lifecycle-based, iterative approach to project delivery. New work begins with initiation: validating the business need, aligning stakeholders, defining success metrics, identifying roles, and creating a lightweight project one-pager or charter. Once the initiative is approved, the team moves into planning, where scope is broken into a prioritized backlog, milestones and release plans are defined, dependencies and risks are captured, and the team agrees on a Definition of Done. This structure helps reduce ambiguity and supports a consistent path from concept to execution.

The project operating model emphasizes clear ownership and cross-functional collaboration. Product managers define outcomes and prioritize the backlog, project managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, and communication, and developers, QA/testing, and stakeholders each contribute according to their responsibilities. OctoAcme also encourages data-informed decisions, regular status visibility, and early escalation of blockers so that issues are addressed before they become major delivery risks. The team operates with a strong emphasis on accountability, transparency, and customer value.

Execution is managed through a clear rhythm of standups, weekly reviews, sprint or milestone demos, and project-board tracking. Work is expected to be broken into manageable increments, with small PRs, acceptance criteria, code review, and CI checks for tests, linting, and security scanning before merge. Ongoing communication keeps teams informed on progress, next steps, risks, and decisions needed, while structured escalation paths help move issues to the appropriate level when they affect delivery or business outcomes.

Release and deployment are treated as controlled, observable events rather than a final handoff. Before release, teams verify that acceptance criteria are met, CI and security checks pass, rollback plans are documented, and smoke tests are ready. After deployment, teams verify outcomes and communicate status to stakeholders. Each sprint, release, or milestone ends with a retrospective to capture what went well, what should improve, and what actions need to be tracked. This continuous improvement loop helps OctoAcme refine processes, reduce single-person dependence, and deliver more predictable results over time.
